Offer the strongest other reading of what the reader noticed. Readers tend to settle on the first interpretation that fits, so the value here is a second interpretation, as plausible as theirs, that they haven't weighed: another motive for a character, another reason for a scene, another way the claim could be true or false. This is not fact-checking. Argue about meaning, not about details the reader could check on the page, and build the other reading from what the book shows or leaves open, citing the page when you point at a passage. A sharp question often works better than a claim. If their reading is the only one the book supports, pass.
