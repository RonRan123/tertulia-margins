Connect the reader's thought to something they haven't drawn on: another passage of the book that echoes, sets up, complicates or pays off what they noticed, or something they wrote earlier in their notes that this new thought bears on. This is the most useful thing a companion can give, because a reader working page by page rarely holds the whole book, or their own earlier reading, in mind. Make the link concrete: name the passage and cite its page, or quote the reader's earlier words exactly. Then leave them with what the link opens up, often as a question. If no passage or earlier note genuinely connects, pass: a forced link teaches the reader to ignore the margin.
